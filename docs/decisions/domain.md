# Domain

- **Task.** A title, optional notes, an optional project, any number of labels, an optional due date with an optional time, an optional recurrence, a priority, a list of reminders, an optional place, an optional calendar event, an optional completion time, and an optional parent task with a position among its siblings.
- **Subtask.** A task with a parent task. It is a full task with its own date, repeat, priority, and reminders, and it nests to any depth. See Subtasks.
- **Recurrence.** Repeats every interval of days, weekdays (Monday to Friday), weeks, months, or years, counted from the due date. A weekly repeat can carry a set of weekdays, such as Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, and then repeats on each of them. Without a set it repeats on the due date's weekday. A recurring task is one task whose due date moves forward. It has no separate history of past occurrences.
- **Due colors.** A due date is tinted by how soon it is: red overdue, orange today, green through 3 days out, purple through a week, then gray (`dueTone`). Under a later day heading a row still shows its full date, as in `Nov 19 9am`; only the Today section shows bare times. The repeat icon has its own orange token.
- **Place and event.** A task can hang off a place it notifies about or shows at, or a calendar event that stands in for its due date. Both are set on iPhone only. See [Places and events](places-and-events.md).
- **Inbox.** The home view. It lists incomplete tasks by the day each sits on (an event's moment, or a repeat at a place skipped forward): Overdue, Today, then each later day under a heading that says only what today leaves ambiguous: `Tomorrow`, a weekday such as `Friday` within the week, then `Sat, Oct 31`, with the year when it differs. Dated tasks from every project show here, subtasks included. Undated tasks live in `No date`. Inbox is a view, not a project. Its badge counts overdue and today's tasks. When nothing is dated it reads `Nothing scheduled` with the hint `Tasks with a date show up here by day.`.
- **No date.** The undated top-level tasks that have no project, in their own view so they do not always sit at the bottom of Inbox. iOS gives it a tab and the web a sidebar entry at `/no-date` with its count, built on `noDateTasks` in `shared/views.ts`. It is one list with no sections, places included, because a backlog reads best as one pile. Tasks sort by when they were added, newest first by default, and each row shows how long ago at its right (`Today`, `3 days ago`, `2 weeks ago`). A `Newest`/`Oldest` button at the top right flips the order and persists per device, because No date is a backlog where age is the useful signal. Like every list, its tab and sidebar entry stay while it is empty (see [Lists](lists.md)). Empty, it reads `Inbox zero` with `Tap + to capture a task.`, or `Press Q to capture a task.` on desktop web.
- **Tomorrow and This week.** Dated views beside Inbox: Tomorrow lists tomorrow's tasks, and This week what is overdue through Sunday, by day like Inbox. See [Lists](lists.md).
- **Postpone.** Moves a task to another day and keeps its time of day. The options follow Todoist: `Today` for an overdue or undated task, `Tomorrow`, `This weekend` (the coming Saturday, or `Next weekend` on a weekend day), and `Next week` (the coming Monday). An option on the task's current day, or on a day an earlier option names, is left out. `postponeOptions` in `shared/format.ts` builds the list for both clients. A toast reads `Moved “X” to <day>` with Undo. Event tasks have no postpone, because the calendar sets their day.
- **Links.** A web URL in a task's title or notes shows as a link wherever the text is read: list rows, subtask rows, search results, and task details while the field is not focused. Focusing a field shows the raw text for editing. A link opens in a new tab on the web and through `openURL` on iOS, and tapping it never opens the task. Only `http(s)://` and `www.` addresses count, plus Markdown `[label](url)` with a web URL, which shows its label because Todoist exports links that way. A bare domain such as `example.com` stays text, since `file.txt` or `v1.2` would read as one. Trailing sentence punctuation and an unopened closing bracket end a bare URL. The text is stored as typed; `linkify` in `shared/links.ts`, ported to `Links.linkify`, splits it for display and carries search highlights onto a Markdown link's label.
- **Completed.** A view of everything the user completed, under the local day each was completed (`Today`, `Yesterday`, then `Mon, Oct 12`), newest first. A range filter picks `Today`, `Last 7 days` (the default), `Last 30 days`, `All time`, or a custom pair of days, both included, and persists per device. Project and label views have a `Show completed` toggle, kept per device and per project or label, so turning it on in one view leaves the others as they were, that lists that view's completed tasks, all time and newest first, below the open ones. A project shows them in place instead: each under its open tasks in its own section, or with the loose tasks when it has none (see [Sections](sections.md)). Each completed row reads its completion moment like a due date (`Today 3:04pm`, `Yesterday 9am`, `Oct 12 3:04pm`) and, when someone other than the signed-in user completed it, `by <name>` with the `AI` badge for an AI account. A null `completedBy` (a completion from before the server stamped them, or one not synced yet) names no one. A subtask completed along with its parent, at the same moment, shows as the parent's progress instead of its own row. The filled checkbox reopens the task, with a `Reopened “X”` toast whose Undo puts back every task the reopen changed, ancestors included. Like the other personal views, Completed reads the user's own space; a shared project or label shows its completed tasks through its toggle. `completedTasks`, `completedDays`, and `completedRange` in `shared/views.ts` and `completedWhen`, `completedHeading`, and `completedRangeLabel` in `shared/format.ts` build it for both clients. The web puts it in the sidebar at `/completed` and the toggle in the view header; iOS puts it at the very bottom of Browse and the toggle in the project and label menus.
- **Project.** A named group of tasks, in an order Stefan sets by dragging. Dragging a project to the edge of the web sidebar scrolls it, so a project can move past the fold in one drag. Every list of projects (the web sidebar, iOS Browse and the Inbox tab's menu, pickers, App Intents) reads `projectsInOrder`, by fractional `order`, then `createdAt`, then `id`, like sections. A new project goes last. Deleting a project deletes its tasks and sections. Deleting a project, section, or label shows a `Deleted “<name>”` toast whose Undo puts back everything the delete removed, tasks, sections, and label assignments included (`deletedBetween` and `undelete` in `shared/store.ts`). Undo keeps edits made in between, and a label returns to its old place in each task's labels. Every project picker ends with `New project`, which creates one and picks it: inline in the web dropdown, and in a name alert on iOS because a native menu cannot hold a text field.
- **Project icon.** A project may carry one icon from a fixed set of 37 concepts (`home`, `work`, `errands`, `money`, `travel`, `learning`, `ideas`, and so on), defined once by name in `PROJECT_ICONS` in `shared/project-icons.ts` and stored as that name in the nullable `projects.icon` column, so it syncs like any other field. A fixed set, not free emoji or any glyph, so both clients always draw the same idea: each maps every name to its own glyph, Phosphor on the web (`app-web/src/lib/project-icon.ts`) and SF Symbols on iOS (`ProjectIcons.swift`), both keyed exhaustively so a new name fails to build until it has a glyph on both. No icon (null, the default for new and imported projects) draws the plain `#` it always had. The icon replaces `#` everywhere a project shows: the web sidebar and phone drawer, the iOS Browse rows and tab bar pins and their switch menus, project pickers and chips, task rows, search, Ramble, and quick add suggestions. A project with an icon also shows it before its title in the project header. It is picked in `Project actions`: a grid of icons led by `No icon` on the web, an `Icon` submenu on iOS. The agent API sets it with `PATCH /api/projects` `{ icon }`. App Intents keep `#`.
- **Section.** A named heading inside one project, in an order Stefan sets. A top-level task sits in at most one section of its project; subtasks sit under their parent. A project view lists the tasks in no section first, then each section. Deleting a section deletes its tasks. See [Sections](sections.md).
- **Label.** A named tag that crosses projects, with a color from a palette of twelve (see Labels UI). A task carries any number of labels, stored as label ids in the order they were added. A label owns no tasks, so deleting a label only takes it off its tasks. Labels with no tasks still exist and still show in the sidebar.
- **Label view.** Every open task carrying a label, across all projects and Inbox, with each row naming its project. Quick add from a label view starts with that label, like every screen's context (see [UX principles](ux-principles.md)).
- **Priority.** Todoist's four levels. `p1` is the most urgent and `p4` is the default with no marking.
- **Reminder.** Either relative to the due time (for example 30 minutes before) or at an absolute date and time. A relative reminder needs a due time to fire. A due time is itself a reminder. Any task with a due time notifies at that time with no reminder set, and reminders add to it, such as 30 minutes before. A date with no time never notifies. A due time at a place does not notify on its own, and an event task notifies at its event moment instead. A task notifies once per moment, so a reminder at the due time does not notify twice. A repeating task notifies at each occurrence's due time, because completing it moves the due date. A reminder is always a system notification, titled with the task and with the due day and time as its body, such as `Today 3:00 PM`; there is no in-app reminder toast. Web Push delivers it on the web and local notifications on iOS, whether the app is open or closed. See Push notifications.
- **Copy as Markdown.** A view, a section, or a task copies to the clipboard as a plain nested list, so a person or an AI gets the gist when it is pasted into notes, a chat, or an LLM. A view is `# Title`, a section is `## Heading`, and each task is `- Title` with its notes, then all its subtasks, nested under it. There are no checkboxes and no metadata: no dates, repeats, priorities, projects, or labels, and done subtasks look like open ones. Each non-blank notes line becomes a nested `- line`, and a notes line that is already a list item (`-`, `*`, `+`, `1.`) keeps its own marker and indent. A project copies its loose tasks first, then each of its sections as `## Section`. A subtask whose ancestor is also copied shows only there. `toMarkdown` in `shared/markdown.ts` builds it for both clients. The web shows a copy button in each view header, on each section heading, and in task details. iOS puts `Copy as Markdown` in row and section-heading context menus, task details, and the project, label, place, and event menus. Both confirm with the toast `Copied as Markdown`.
