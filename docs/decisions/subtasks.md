# Subtasks

- **Subtasks are tasks.** A subtask is a `Task` with a `parentId` and an `order` among its siblings, not a checklist item. It has its own date, repeat, priority, reminders, notes, and subtasks, so nesting has no depth limit. This matches Todoist, and it keeps one shape for reminders, search, and sync.
- **Where they show.** Project lists and `No date` show top-level tasks only, and a parent row shows a progress ring with `done/total` for its direct subtasks. The Inbox's dated sections list a dated subtask as its own row, marked with its parent's title, because a date is a commitment on its own.
- **Adding in quick add.** Quick add takes subtask lines under the title and saves them with the new task. See [Quick add](quick-add.md).
- **Editing.** Task details lists the subtasks under the notes, with a field that adds one on Enter and keeps focus for the next. The field parses dates, repeats, priority, reminders, and `@` labels like quick add. It has no `#` project, and a `#Name` in a subtask's title stays text, because a subtask always shares its root task's project, and moving a parent moves every task under it. Tapping a subtask opens its details in the same sheet, sliding in like a navigation push, and a back chip with the parent's title replaces the project picker. Going back returns to where the parent was scrolled, on the web as on iOS. Opening a task afresh starts at the top.
- **Task details scroll.** The date, repeat, priority, and reminder chips sit under the title, outside the scrolling body, so their menus never clip. The notes and subtasks scroll on every screen size, so a long checklist never pushes `Add subtask` off a phone screen. `Complete` and `Delete` float over the bottom corners, and the body pads its end to clear them.
- **Titles wrap.** A subtask row shows its whole title over as many lines as it needs, because a subtask is often a note to self that loses its point when cut off.
- **Reorder.** Drag the handle on a subtask row, by mouse or touch, or focus it and press the up and down arrows. The rows it passes slide out of its way, and it settles into place on release. Order persists. A move writes only the moved subtask's fractional `order`, so moves on two devices do not overwrite each other (see [Sync](sync.md#conflicts)).
- **Completing a parent completes everything under it.** Checking off a task completes every open subtask under it, at every depth. Undo puts back exactly the tasks the completion changed, and only their completion, date, and reminders, so subtasks that were already done stay done and edits made since survive. An open task never sits under a completed one, so reopening a subtask, or adding one, reopens its completed parents.
- **Checking a subtask in task details toggles it.** The checkbox is its own undo, so there is no toast. Completing a subtask with `Complete` in its own details returns to the parent.
- **Recurring parents reset their subtasks.** Completing a recurring task moves it to its next occurrence and reopens every subtask under it, so each occurrence starts with a fresh checklist. Subtask dates do not move with the parent. Undo restores the parent's date and every subtask's state.
- **Reminders stay per task.** A subtask notifies at its own due time and reminders, like any task, while it is open. Completing a parent completes its subtasks, which silences theirs. Subtasks do not inherit the parent's reminders.
- **Search finds subtasks by their own text.** A subtask is a result like any task, matched on its own title and notes, never its parent's. Its row names its parent under the title, and opening it shows the back chip to the parent.
- **Labels are per task.** A new subtask starts with only the labels typed into it and never inherits or follows its parent's, unlike the project. A label marks the task it is on, so a label view lists only the tasks that carry it. This matches Todoist.
- **Turning subtasks into tasks.** The `…` menu on the Subtasks heading in task details turns a task's direct subtasks into tasks of their own, in one of three ways: keep their titles, start each with the parent's title (`Plan trip: Book flights`), or inherit the parent's labels, date, and priority wherever a subtask has none. Inheriting copies the date but not the repeat, because the repeat is the parent's routine, and it leaves a subtask on a calendar event alone. The project needs no copying, since a subtask already shares its root's. The parent stays, now without children, because it often still means something on its own, and deleting it is one tap away. Each subtask moves up one level and keeps its own subtasks: a top-level parent's become top-level tasks, and a nested parent's sit right after it among its siblings. Completed subtasks move too, so the parent is left with none. The toast's `Undo` writes back only the fields it changed (parent, order, title, labels, date, and priority). A subtask whose parent was deleted since stays where it is, so it never goes unreachable. The action shows only on tasks in the user's own space: moving a subtask up can carry it out of what a share grants (a shared task's subtasks, or a label share's unlabeled ones), the server would refuse the edit, and a guest's client cannot see the grant to tell. It is a store command in `shared/store.ts` (`explodeSubtasks`) with a Swift port, and it syncs as ordinary field edits. Ramble's parse-time promotion, which drops a parent with no title, works on a parse tree rather than stored tasks, so the two share no code.
- **Delete takes the subtree.** Deleting a task deletes every subtask under it, and undo restores all of them. Deleting a project deletes its tasks, subtasks included.
- **Stored data.** Tasks stored before subtasks existed load as top-level tasks. A subtask whose parent is missing, or whose parents loop, loads as a top-level task so it never goes unreachable.
